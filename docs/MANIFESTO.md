# Deckard Manifesto

Your money should obey you. Not a bank, not an exchange, not a company that can freeze
your account, debank you, or change the terms while you sleep.

Self-custody is the answer to that, and self-custody is a part-time job. Get the income
off the exchange. Set aside runway in something that holds its value and cannot be
switched off. Put the rest to work. Cover what is recurring. Do not fat-finger an
approval and lose everything. Do it again next week.

So most people give up and hand their money back to a custodian. They trade their
sovereignty for convenience, because being your own bank is exhausting and frightening.

That trade is the problem. We do not want to choose between sovereign and usable.

Deckard is the missing piece: an agent that works for you and only you. It runs on your
machine. It holds your keys in the secure enclave. It reads your situation locally,
nothing leaves your device. And it can never be turned against you, because it physically
cannot move your money outside the rules you set. The agent proposes. Cryptography
enforces. You hold the keys.

We are not asking you to trust us. We are asking you to trust math and your own keys.
That is the whole point. Every other "private" or "smart" money tool eventually asks you
to trust a company, a server, a jurisdiction. Companies get bought, compelled, breached,
and bored. Math does not. Open source means you can check, instead of believe.

The goal is money that runs itself and stays yours. We are not there on day one. Money
software earns trust before it earns autonomy, so Deckard starts by proposing every move
for your one-tap approval, and graduates to hands-off only after an audit and a second
set of expert eyes. The autonomy is the soul of this thing. We are going to earn it in
the open.

This is for the people who already live onchain and refuse to go back. If that is you,
come build it, break it, and tell me where it is wrong.

Sovereignty you do not have to think about. That is the bar.
